# PSE commercial balancing, July 2026

**Source:** PSE Balancing Market Monitoring Office, *Bilansowanie handlowe KSE*, July 2026, 40 pages; [official PDF](https://www.pse.pl/documents/20182/7206488403/Bilansowanie+handlowe+POB+-+lipiec+2026+r.pdf/5e324f31-1fc5-483c-803c-b209175c4c9f?safeargs=646f776e6c6f61643d74727565), published 2026-08-31. All pages read and rendered charts visually inspected on 2026-11-16. Relevant to [Polish reserve procurement and incentives](../polish-balancing-capacity-procurement.md#incentives).

### Findings

- **Persistent behaviour:** pp. 6–10 compare actual portfolios, including renewable producers, showing contracting that follows physical demand/generation versus recurring departures without corrective intraday trading. Page 8 describes a participant exceeding 550 MW imbalance. Pages 33–35 also show individual improvement from 2025 to 2026, despite PSE's aggregate no-clear-improvement conclusion on p. 12. These are selected examples, not estimates of the causal contribution of each behaviour.
- **Shortage and surplus are different cases:** p. 15 attributes the 22 July evening shortfall to insufficient earlier purchases, with unused import potential, extra generation starts and imbalance energy costing more than twice planned purchases. Page 16 instead shows 28–30 July unplanned **deliveries** reaching/exceeding 2 GW and renewable curtailment, alongside similar photovoltaic forecasts and output. PSE says these deliveries exceed contracted reserves severalfold. That is surplus/downward-balancing evidence; it does not directly establish upward scarcity behind an export cap. The chart does not plot a contracted-reserve series or identify the compared reserve product.
- **Concentration:** p. 18's ten anonymised balancing responsible parties have 38–101 GWh each of gross monthly imbalance; the share of periods above 100 MW ranges from 14% to 53%. Pages 20–29 juxtapose their signed profiles with the system series. They reveal recurring mismatches but do not identify the firms or their motives.
- **Financial incentives:** p. 31 estimates July savings of PLN 40 million for those ten parties and PLN 82 million for all parties, assuming 20% of imbalance remains settled at CEN, 40% moves to intraday prices and 40% to day-ahead prices. Some daily bars are negative. These are hypothetical savings at the assumed price mix, not audited realised losses or a market rerun demonstrating that all replacement trades were feasible at unchanged prices. Page 38 projects roughly PLN 700 million annual forgone benefits; it does not present a separate calculation supporting the annual figure.
- **Policy position:** pp. 2–3 assert reserve exhaustion and seek stronger balancing incentives, including an incentive pricing component. Page 39 says tools are being analysed and consulted through the MAPRE energy-market roadmap. This establishes a proposal, not implementation or a measured effect.

### Reading

Page 19 defines positive values as surplus and negative values as shortage. The system contracting state is the sum of party imbalances adjusted for non-market operator exchange and redispatch. Darker sections mark system imbalance magnitude above 1,000 MW, not proof that a particular party worsened it: compare the signs. Party plots use different scales from the system plot and often omit numerical party-axis ticks. Exact peaks cannot be digitised confidently from those plots.

Pages 4 and 6 reproduce the alternative of helping balance the system, rather than requiring every party's individual imbalance to be zero. This matters when interpreting a participant acting against the aggregate imbalance.

### Limits

This is evidence of portfolio mismatch, price exposure and PSE's diagnosis. No chart matches live reserve dispatch to procurement by product; none plots allocation-cap values or tests their necessity. Page 14 discusses foregone cross-border flows from energy absent from earlier markets, and p. 15 discusses unused imports. Neither proves that tightening a specific day-ahead export cap is the efficient remedy.
