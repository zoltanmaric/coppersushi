# Core day-ahead capacity calculation: fourth-amendment explanatory document

**Source:** Core transmission system operators, December 2025, [29-page PDF hosted by ACER](https://www.acer.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/Media/News/Documents/Explanatory-document-DA-CCM-CCR-2026.pdf). Read on 2026-11-12. Explains a proposal; it is not the approval decision or proof of implementation. See the [2018 note](core-da-ccm-explanatory-note.md) for the original process and legal-document links.

### Poland

Section 3 (printed pp. 1–5) explains [allocation constraints](../core-day-ahead-capacity-calculation.md#allocation-constraint) through reserve availability. Poland’s integrated scheduling process follows day-ahead clearing. Unrestricted energy sales can exhaust upward reserves; imports can exhaust downward flexibility. The document reports insufficient offers in the balancing-capacity market launched on 14 June 2024, despite fewer binding caps. Its welfare claims report PSE’s analysis, not independent validation.

This rationale does not establish that a particular 0 MW limit was necessary, optimally sized, or economically efficient. The [country-cap analysis](../country-import-export-caps.md) owns observed limits and price attribution.

### Scope

Section 7 proposes Irish net-position caps and separate Celtic ramping limits; it does not establish their operational start. Sections 8–9 revise transfer sensitivities and propose a harmonised [generation shift key](../core-day-ahead-capacity-calculation.md#gsk), with better-performing alternatives allowed. Section 10 explains conditional removal of long-term allocation inclusion and replacement fallbacks. Sections 2, 4–6 cover security limits, Hungary’s lower-voltage grid, German internal direct-current links and Celtic integration.
