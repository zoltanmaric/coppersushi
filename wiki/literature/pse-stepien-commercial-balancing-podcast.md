# Mateusz Stępień on commercial balancing

**Source:** PSE's *Pod najwyższym napięciem*, interview by Maciej Wapiński with Mateusz Stępień, director of the Balancing Market Monitoring Office; [YouTube episode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KgJQIfLKKQc), published 2026-03-24, duration 26:01. The full available Polish auto-generated transcript (00:02–25:56, including closing remarks) was read on 2026-11-16; audio was not independently checked. Captions contain obvious transcription errors, so precise wording and numbers need that qualification. The episode predates July: its description links the January 2026 report. PSE's July announcement links back to it.

The video description expressly presents the podcast as educational expert opinion, not the company's official position. Attribute the following to Stępień; the [July report](pse-commercial-balancing-july-2026.md) is a separate source.

### Behaviour

- [10:07–12:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KgJQIfLKKQc&t=607s): he distinguishes unavoidable forecast errors from participants failing to monitor and correct persistent deviations. His explanation is slow learning and organisational/formal adaptation to round-the-clock operation after the 2024 reform. He says some parties decline available tools or have not learned to use them, and sees insufficient attempts to capture savings. This is his diagnosis, not a quantified causal study or proof that deliberate imbalance trading is profitable.
- [12:43–14:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KgJQIfLKKQc&t=763s): he associates the delay with Poland's history of central dispatch and operator responsibility. His operational criterion for poor balancing is a repeated deviation followed by no corrective action in later periods, rather than the mere existence of an error.
- [16:50–18:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KgJQIfLKKQc&t=1010s): he says consumers ultimately bear costs through both network tariffs and balancing-responsible-party charges. This supports cost pass-through as a stated economic channel; it does not identify individual contracts, establish full marginal pass-through, or prove that it causes inaction.

### Alpha

[23:01–25:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KgJQIfLKKQc&t=1381s): he describes a proposed motivating coefficient, alpha, to strengthen incentives for earlier contracting. His distinctive argument is predictability: participants can anticipate the adjustment before deciding whether to trade, whereas a realised scarcity spike is uncertain beforehand. He says the mechanism is symmetric and that participants with reasonably balanced errors should not be heavily burdened because payments and receipts can offset. The episode provides neither its formula nor an implementation decision or measured behavioural effect. This pricing coefficient is not the flow-based market-coupling alpha used elsewhere in the project.

### Reserves

- [04:40–05:11 and 10:38–11:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KgJQIfLKKQc&t=280s): he describes purchased reserves as finite, with neighbouring-operator assistance and interventions as further options. Reserves are intended to cover residual random deviations after participants adjust their portfolios.
- [19:24–21:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KgJQIfLKKQc&t=1164s): he attributes a past extreme positive-price episode to a rapid increase in balancing demand, calling on Polish and neighbouring PICASSO resources and the most expensive offers. His description explicitly involves increasing generation: an **upward** event, distinct from the July report's surplus/downward example. He alternates between exhaustion and being close to the available limit; he supplies no event date, product-level procurement/activation comparison or high-resolution dispatch trace. His claim that better contracting would probably have avoided it is not a reproduced counterfactual.
- [21:50–23:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KgJQIfLKKQc&t=1310s): he distinguishes purchased capacity/readiness from delivered balancing energy. This supports the project's separation of MW procurement and MWh activation.

### Limits

The transcript does not discuss Polish allocation/export-cap calculations or demonstrate the necessity of a specific cap. Claims about cheaper wholesale prices and savings from earlier trading are explanatory counterfactuals, not market reruns. Its statement at 02:57–03:12 about prices becoming known four days after delivery must not be used as the current API publication contract: the project's sampled records include next-day publication and later revisions. Its 2026-03-24 proposals and operational descriptions are not evidence of implementation as of November.
