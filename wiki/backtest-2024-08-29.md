# Backtest: one solved day against the market

**Verdict: the model orders zonal price spreads with real skill once carbon is priced.** Rank
correlation went from none to +0.29 across the day and above +0.42 in five afternoon and evening
hours. Price levels remain low and congestion *location* is still uncorrelated.

Day: **2024-08-29**, the [sushi-2](specs/sushi-2.md) demo day. Model: the OSM grid, 4390 buses,
7371 lines, 12 two-hour snapshots. Truth: JAO's published binding CNECs (78 that day) and settled
day-ahead prices for the twelve Core zones.

## What changed, and what it bought

| Run | Change | Objective | Price bias | Price MAE | **Zone-pair rho** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | baseline (static costs, no carbon) | 154.5 M€ | −118.9 | 118.9 | **−0.03** |
| 2 | `conventional.dynamic_fuel_price` | 178.4 M€ | −109.8 | 109.8 | **−0.02** |
| 3 | `costs.emission_prices.dynamic` | 296.9 M€ | −62.3 | 67.0 | **+0.28** |
| 4 | `costs.year: 2025` (was 2050) | 306.7 M€ | −60.4 | 65.7 | **+0.29** |

Bias and MAE are €/MWh against settled prices. Rho is the Spearman correlation between the 66 Core
zone pairs ranked by predicted price gap and by settled spread — the metric the Core congestion forecast scores on.

**Carbon is the whole story.** Pricing CO2 moved rho from nothing to +0.28 and halved the bias;
fuel prices and the cost vintage were worth ~1 point of bias each.

## What helped

- **`costs.emission_prices.dynamic: true`** — Instrat's daily EUA series (€69.50/t on the day),
  so it follows whatever day is solved. Without it carbon is free and coal sets the price at €25.
- **`conventional.dynamic_fuel_price: true`** — monthly World Bank prices instead of static
  assumptions. Needs a fork patch: upstream's `reindex(snapshots).ffill()` yields all-NaN costs for
  a window not starting on a month boundary ([rehearsal PR](https://github.com/zoltanmaric/pypsa-eur/pull/6),
  [ledger](upstream-contributions.md)). `emission_prices.dynamic` additionally needs the pandas-3
  `read_json` fix ([rehearsal PR](https://github.com/zoltanmaric/pypsa-eur/pull/7)), without which the
  CO2-price rule fails outright.
- **`costs.year: 2025`** — the nearest published vintage to the day; upstream defaults to 2050.
  `costs_2024.csv` does not exist; technology-data ships five-year steps.
- **`atlite.default_cutout: europe-2024-sarah3-era5`** — correctness, not accuracy: the weather
  year must follow the day ([pypsa-eur-sibling](pypsa-eur-sibling.md)).
- **`solving.options.assign_all_duals: true`** — off by default, and without it `lines_t` holds
  only `p0`/`p1`, so there are no line shadow prices to compare with JAO's.
- **`load.supplement_synthetic: false`** — unblocks a post-2023 day; the synthetic series ends
  2023-12-31. The trade-off: measured demand is now the only source, so a country-hour with a gap
  fails the script's own `load.isna()` assertion instead of being filled. Nothing was lost for the
  countries we added — UA, MD, XK, CY and MT are excluded from the synthetic dataset by name and always
  came from measured series. Restoring it for a post-2023 day means running the calculator
  ([Zenodo 10820928](https://zenodo.org/records/10820928)) to extend the series, which we punted on.

## What did not move

**Congestion location.** Country-level rank correlation between the model's binding lines and JAO's
binding CNECs stayed at ≈0 through every run (−0.03 to −0.07). The model reports 45–80 lines at
their cap per snapshot against JAO's 0–10, and places congestion in France (47, JAO 0) and Germany
(59, JAO 22) while missing Austria (0, JAO 25). Costs cannot fix this; it is what
[jao-grid](specs/jao-grid.md) targets.

**Where the level bias sits.** North-west Europe is within €17–46 (AT −17, FR −21, DE −27); the
south-east is €99–127 low (SI −127, RO −112, HR −112). The model prices all twelve zones in one
76–97 €/MWh band while the market split into a ~110 north-west and a ~200 south-east.

Regional ranking, measured on run 3: all twelve zones **+0.28**, north-west only **+0.32**,
south-east only **+0.62**. Skill inside each region is good; the combined figure is dragged down by
cross-region pairs. The residual is one regional level offset, not twelve zonal errors — so
scoping a demo to north-west Europe would drop the better-ordered half.

## To raise accuracy further

1. **Generator outages** (ENTSO-E 15.1.A/B). The model runs every unit at full availability. On the
   day it dispatched **+153 GWh of French nuclear (+16%)** and +11 GWh Hungarian (+33%) that did not
   run — cheap energy depressing prices across the coupled region. Romanian nuclear matched to 3%,
   so this is fleet-wide, not one plant.
2. **Unit commitment** (`conventional.unit_commitment: true`). PyPSA implements it — `committable`,
   `min_up_time`, `min_down_time`, start-up costs — and PyPSA-Eur already ships
   `data/unit_commitment.csv`. Without it the LP switches the lignite fleet off entirely: at spot
   carbon, lignite's €108/MWh sits above CCGT's €85, so the model burned 17 GWh against Germany's
   actual 194 GWh. Real lignite is must-run and does not cycle off for one day. Cost is MILP solve
   time at 4390 buses — worth one timed experiment before assuming it is out of reach.
3. **True limits and N-1** — designed in [jao-grid](specs/jao-grid.md), which owns this lever. The only lever that addresses congestion
   location: JAO's own `fmax` in place of PyPSA-Eur's 2000 MVA transformer default, and explicit
   contingency constraints in place of the blanket 0.7 cap.
4. **Hourly snapshots.** Two-hour resolution is compared against hourly truth.
5. **South-east scarcity.** Unexplained; not Romanian nuclear. Candidates: hydro reservoir levels
   after a dry summer, or Balkan interconnector limits.
6. **More days.** This is n=1.

## Reproducing

Truth data needs no key: JAO's publication tool (`shadowPrices`, no auth) and
[energy-charts](https://api.energy-charts.info) for settled prices and actual generation.

The scripts that produced these numbers — the fetchers, the scorecard, the model-versus-actual
comparison — are parked unmerged in [PR #41](https://github.com/zoltanmaric/coppersushi/pull/41),
with hardcoded paths and no tests. They are not a foundation, but they are the record of the method:
the starting point for porting scoring into the package, and for running the next backtest.

**Settled prices came from energy-charts, not Electricity Maps.** The trial key returns 401 on every
`past`/`past-range` endpoint, so it cannot serve a historical day at all; it also lacks
`power-breakdown/forecast`. energy-charts is free, unauthenticated, and covers the twelve Core zones
hourly — but not every zone (HR and SI are absent from its generation endpoint) and it rate-limits.

Neither source dominates: Electricity Maps has the coverage and the forecasts, energy-charts has the
history without a key. So the price and generation adapters should take the source from
configuration rather than hard-coding one, and a backtest and a live forecast can then draw from
whichever suits. Nothing implements this yet.
