# Reviewing a day-ahead price forecast

Start with the decision the forecast supports. Who needs which prices, for which market and delivery intervals, by what local time? What can that person still do if a forecast is late, incomplete or wrong? A research replay can establish historical skill. A claim about daily service also needs evidence from the edition a customer could retrieve before acting. [DE–LU forecast contract](germany-luxembourg.md#contract) · [Operational evidence](operational-forecasting.md)

## What can change a verdict?

**Observed example.** Over 2–8 October 2026, Eiser’s 11:00 German-time edition had a [weighted interval score (WIS)](evaluation.md#wis) of 11.44 €/MWh. Its noon edition scored 7.16 on the same 672 quarters against the same price observations. The participant switches to an early-auction price model after 11:00. Only the earlier edition was usable at 11:00. Even the 11:00 edition’s weekly 50% interval coverage of 57.74% hid a day with just 15 of 96 prices inside the interval. This supports comparing editions at the customer’s cutoff and inspecting individual days. It establishes neither the cause of the gain nor long-run calibration. ## Review questions

These tests are derived from the linked evidence. Pick one delivery day and trace it from source records through the ordinary forecast command to the output the customer reads. Then change one condition at a time.

| Question | Evidence to request or test to run | What a failure means |
|---|---|---|
| Which input edition and which truth? | For a revised input, inspect delivery time, source issue time, actual receipt and the exact version selected. Replay just before and after the revision. Identify the price series and revision used for training at fit time and the frozen truth used for scoring. Label historical availability as witnessed, documented, assumed or unknown. [Point-in-time evidence](data-sources.md#point-in-time) | A replay can give the forecaster information the customer lacked at decision time. A change of scoring truth can move the comparison. |
| Can a join or fit see the future? | Add a post-cutoff revision and a later training label. The earlier features and predictions should stay identical. Inspect UTC row keys, units, geography and interval meaning. Trace a repeated or missing local-clock quarter through the lag join and verify the complete delivery grid. Check that preprocessing and calibration use only eligible training data. [Replay rules](evaluation.md#replay) · [Weather alignment](data-sources.md#weather) | A plausible-looking forecast can use a later observation, shift weather by an hour or silently drop a tradable quarter. |
| What skill belongs to the model, and what reached the customer? | Compare candidate and simple reference on identical dates, truth, issue time and output shape. For a model claim, hold inputs fixed. For a service claim, enumerate every expected day, edition delivered, fallback, late or missing output and missing truth. Score what was delivered and show delivery coverage alongside the score. Inspect paired differences across contiguous days. [Controls and uncertainty](evaluation.md) · [Participant scoring rules](operational-forecasting.md) | A strong score on successful model runs can hide the days a customer had only a fallback or no forecast. |
| Do the uncertainty ranges mean what the user needs? | Check finite, ordered [price quantiles](evaluation.md#quantiles). Alongside WIS, show interval widths and counts below, inside and above each band, including by delivery hour and on difficult days. Show sample counts. For a decision that depends on a whole price path, ask what evidence supports dependence across quarters. [Evaluation limits](evaluation.md#limitations) · [Daily path risk](germany-luxembourg.md#continuation) | Narrow bands can understate exposure in particular hours. Per-quarter coverage alone cannot validate a battery schedule. |
| Can the operator explain a release and reverse it? | Identify code, fitted model, inputs and published edition separately. If model and provider both change, compare complete old and new systems, then compare models on common inputs. For a live release, restore the previous approved version through the normal route and read its next output. Check edition identity and retained history. [ECMWF release comparison and release identities](operational-forecasting.md) · [Snapshot identity](forecast-infrastructure.md#snapshot) | An apparent model gain may come from a new feed. A rollback that restores only weights may still serve incompatible features or obscure what a customer saw. |
| Can the operator detect and recover from a missed deadline? | Delay one required feed, interrupt a write, then retry after a simulated lost acknowledgement. Inspect the consumer’s view for partial or duplicate editions. Check that a named operator can distinguish fresh, fallback and missed delivery, find the failing step and tell whether recovery can still help the customer. [Publication and retry evidence](operational-forecasting.md) · [Fallback contract](evaluation.md#defaults) | A green job can leave yesterday’s forecast visible or send two conflicting editions. |

## How much architecture does the decision justify?

- **Research replay.** Ask another reviewer to reproduce one score from fixed inputs, code, environment and settings through the normal command. A local batch run with durable inputs, predictions and a report can answer this. A scheduler adds little evidence about model skill. [Storage selection principle](forecast-infrastructure.md#selection-principle)
- **Prospective trial.** Record actual input arrival, selected versions, issue time and output before the decision. Compare each edition with later truth, including late and missed days. This tests historical timing assumptions and measures how much of the deadline is spent waiting for data. [Historical availability limits](data-sources.md#availability) · [Weather arrival example](operational-forecasting.md)
- **Customer-facing service.** Trace a reader-visible deadline miss and a rollback before accepting an availability claim. For each additional service or defensive branch, ask which failure it handles, how often it occurs, what the customer loses and how long recovery takes with the simpler design. Use a measured bottleneck or a stated service obligation to justify the component. [Deployment stages](forecast-infrastructure.md) · [Architecture review criteria](../../.agents/skills/pull-request/SKILL.md#review-findings)

Record a finding as a decision the evidence supports, the exact artifact or failed probe, the customer consequence and the smallest change that would resolve it. Mark an unexercised path as unverified. Passing a research replay and meeting a daily delivery commitment are separate verdicts.
