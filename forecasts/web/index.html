THE DAILY VIEW

What do these forecast methods mean?

Three trained models use the same CatBoost approach, which learns relationships from past examples. They differ in their inputs. The reference is a simple comparison method; automatic selection chooses a method rather than fitting another model.

Automatic selection (with fallbacks)
Uses the price-history + weather + demand model when inputs and execution permit. Otherwise falls back to weather, price history, then the reference. The method actually used on this day is shown below the chart.
Price-history model
Uses previous prices at the same time of day, recent daily price summaries, and calendar information such as weekday and holidays.
Price-history + weather model
Adds forecast temperature, wind and solar radiation from twelve locations to the price-history model’s inputs.
Price-history + weather + demand model
Also adds forecast electricity demand for Germany, with indicators for missing inputs.
Yesterday / last-week reference
Averages yesterday’s and last week’s same-clock prices. Uses either when only one is available, or an earlier same-quarter median when neither is available. Its bands come from past forecast errors in the training data.

All five are saved historical predictions. Training expands monthly using earlier eligible data. If an individual trained model fails, its displayed prediction uses the reference.

THE CONTRACT

What is being predicted?

One electricity price for each quarter-hour of the next German local day, in euros per megawatt-hour. The information deadline is 11:00 Europe/Berlin on the preceding day.

The implemented result is a historical evaluation with monthly refits. Scheduled daily issuance remains planned. Forecast contract ↗

Why five lines?

The median is the central prediction. The 25th and 75th percentiles enclose a nominal 50% range; the 2.5th and 97.5th enclose a nominal 95% range.

Those percentages are targets, not proof of calibration. The observed coverage alongside the chart tells you how often prices actually fell inside. These ranges apply to individual quarters, not the joint path of a whole day. Quantile contract ↗

THE CENTRAL CAVEAT

A narrow band can look confident.
Coverage tells us whether it earned that confidence.

What counts as a good forecast?

Weighted interval score (WIS) balances band width against penalties for prices outside the bands, together with median error. Lower is better. A good aggregate score can coexist with poor coverage.

MAE is the median prediction's average absolute error. RMSE penalises large errors more heavily. Both use that same median prediction. Compare coverage with nominal 50% and 95%, and inspect band widths alongside it. Scoring contract ↗

What is “the reference”?

A historical-price forecast: average yesterday's and last week's prices at the same German local quarter. Use the available one if the other is missing; if both are absent, use a training-history quarter median.

Add quantiles of training-only historical errors to form its bands, recalculated at each monthly fit. It is a local reference, not an external provider's forecast. Reference definition ↗

What does the model see?

Past prices and calendar features, forecast weather across twelve locations, and eligible demand forecasts. Three fixed feature sets expose the effect of adding weather and demand.

The system chooses demand → weather → prices/calendar → reference when entire sources or model executions fail. Partial gaps remain missing values. The choice never uses realised prices or test scores. Fallback contract ↗

What can this evidence establish?

It measures a retrospective evaluation on a fixed snapshot. Historical price and weather availability rely on assumptions; demand timing is conditional on its update timestamp. Reproducibility does not establish historical availability.

Some evaluation dates were inspected in earlier development. Daily readiness, calibrated extreme-price risk and trading profitability are not established. Source limitations ↗
